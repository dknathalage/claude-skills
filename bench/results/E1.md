## Verdict

**At the threshold.** The swarm beat the same tasks run one at a time by **29.6% on median wall-clock** (401 s vs 570 s), against a 30% bar. Paired by rep it was 34.7%, 32.7% and 29.6% (median 32.7%). Cost was equal ($1.48 vs $1.50) and quality was equal (20/20 hidden tests, review 8). Strictly the median-vs-median rule misses by 0.4 points; the paired comparison clears it. Don decides.

**Against superpowers**, on the same plan: **42% faster** (401 s vs 695 s), **62% cheaper** ($1.48 vs $3.88), same hidden pass rate (100%), review one point lower (8 vs 9).

## What the numbers say

- **Parallelism has little room on this graph.** The waves are 2 → 5 → 1, so the best possible speedup over serial is small. F-large (widest wave 12) is the real test of parallelism.
- **Opus is most of the swarm's cost:** $1.12–1.66 per run, against $0.15–0.19 for all Haiku work. That covers reading the plan, starting the workflow, the whole-branch review (which runs on the session model) and the final check. A cheaper review model is the obvious next lever (E3 territory).
- **Superpowers spends on Sonnet** ($1.43–2.02 per run, its per-task reviewers) as well as Opus.

## Caveats

- **Superpowers was refused 20–27 commands per run.** Mostly these were `cd` into its scratchpad or worktrees outside the project, and its own helper scripts, under the benchmark's allowlist. That probably cost it some minutes. Before FINAL the allowlist is widened the same way for both arms.
- **Timing noise in rep 1:** swarm rep 1 overlapped the F-large fixture build (dotnet on the same machine). Rep 1 was the slowest swarm run (519 s); reps 2–3 alone give 31% faster than serial.
- n = 3 per arm. Serial ranged 510–795 s and swarm 343–519 s, so the spread is wide.
